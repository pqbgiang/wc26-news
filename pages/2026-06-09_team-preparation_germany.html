Is Germany Match-Ready?

Tuesday, June 9, 2026

🏥 Current Roster & Injuries

Lennart Karl ruled out with torn muscle

Lennart Karl withdrew injured and was replaced by Assan Ouédraogo in Germany's 26-man squad. Manuel Neuer's calf injury kept him out of pre-World Cup friendlies against Finland and the USA, but all indications are that the 40-year-old will be fit to face Curaçao on June 14.

Key players available and ready

Jamal Musiala has recovered well from a broken leg suffered at the FIFA Club World Cup and has been getting better with still plenty of time to build up rhythm. Florian Wirtz has already hit double digits for goals and assists for his nation.

Serge Gnabry unavailable

Serge Gnabry's injury setback means Leroy Sané could be asked to provide width and pace on the right. Gnabry suffered a torn adductor and is unavailable for the tournament.

💪 Fitness & Form

Strong recent performances

Julian Nagelsmann's side eased to World Cup 2026 qualification with five wins from six. Germany also performed well as the host nation at Euro 2024, falling to Spain in the quarterfinals.

Club form transitions

Deniz Undav finished as the leading German scorer in the Bundesliga in 2025/26 on 19 goals, with Nagelsmann stating 'You cannot leave a striker like him at home.' Florian Wirtz has contributed 15 goal involvements in all competitions for Liverpool.

Optimistic mood heading to tournament

The mood around the camp has shifted considerably under Nagelsmann, with genuine optimism heading into North America. The mood around the squad seems optimistic, and they will expect to go deep in the tournament.

📋 Tactical Setup

4-2-3-1 formation preferred

The lone striker's role in Nagelsmann's preferred 4-2-3-1 formation remains open, with Kai Havertz appearing to be the preferred option when fit, while Nick Woltemade's height and physical presence offer a different approach.

Midfield and defense structure

Leon Goretzka should link arms with highly-rated Aleksandar Pavlovic in centre-midfield. Jonathan Tah and Nico Schlotterbeck are expected to form a centre-back partnership in a four-man defence.

Pressing and possession focus

Nagelsmann's style relies on gegenpressing when his team lose the ball, while on the ball his teams often try to dominate possession.

🎯 Group E Preview

First fixture: Curaçao on June 14

Germany vs. Curaçao at Houston Stadium on June 14. Curaçao are the smallest nation ever to reach a World Cup.

✅ CONFIRMED

Second match: Ivory Coast on June 20

Germany face Côte d'Ivoire on June 20. The Ivorians, returning to the World Cup for the first time in 12 years, are a dangerous side and boast the likes of Amad Diallo.

✅ CONFIRMED

Final group game: Ecuador on June 25

Germany face Ecuador on June 25 in the group finale. Ecuador are a disciplined, well drilled side who conceded just five goals in 18 qualifiers and could be hard for Germany to break down.

✅ CONFIRMED

🏆 Tournament Expectations

Favorable Group E draw

Germany will likely have breathed a sigh of relief when the draw for Group E was made, as on paper they will expect to progress comfortably. The group consists of Germany, Curaçao, Ivory Coast, and Ecuador.

Redemption mission after recent exits

Germany had never failed to advance from consecutive FIFA men's World Cup group stages until four years ago, but Nagelsmann has instilled a new standard into the squad. Germany will fully expect to be in the knockout stages in a World Cup for the first time since 2014 when they went on to lift the trophy.

Squad strength and championship pedigree

Germany will be regarded as one of the favourites to progress to the latter stages of the tournament despite recent form in this competition. The joint-most successful European side in World Cups with four wins, Germany are looking to match Brazil's record of five successes at the finals.
