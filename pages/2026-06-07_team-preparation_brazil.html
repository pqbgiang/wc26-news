The final matchday of Group D brings one of international football's most storied rivalries to North America. 1. Opponent Analysis: Bielsa's High-Pressing 🇺🇾 Uruguay

Under Marcelo Bielsa, 🇺🇾 Uruguay operates in a highly intense 4-3-3 system that morphs into a 3-1-3-3 during build-up phases. Their primary weapon is a relentless, man-oriented press designed to disrupt the opponent's first phase of progression. They seek to win the ball high up the pitch and transition immediately through vertical passes targeting Darwin Núñez, who excels at running the channels.

The midfield engine is powered by Federico Valverde, who acts as a box-to-box progressor, while Manuel Ugarte provides the defensive screening. Uruguay's main vulnerability lies in the space left behind their aggressive, high-pressing full-backs, particularly when their central defenders are forced to shift wide to cover.

To nullify 🇺🇾 Uruguay's vertical threats, 🇧🇷 Brazil must establish a robust "rest defense" structure. Dorival Júnior is projected to deploy a 4-2-3-1 base, utilizing a double pivot of Bruno Guimarães and João Gomes. Gomes will act as the primary destroyer, specifically tasked with tracking Valverde's late runs into the box.

  • Preventing Central Progression: Marquinhos and Gabriel Magalhães must maintain a compact distance to squeeze the space behind the double pivot, denying Núñez room to turn.
  • Full-back Restraint: Danilo will likely play a more inverted, conservative role on the right, forming a back-three in possession to prevent 🇺🇾 Uruguay's rapid 3v3 counter-attacking scenarios.

This tactical bait will open up diagonal corridors for Vinícius Júnior to exploit. By drawing Ronald Araújo out of the defensive line, Vinícius Júnior can use his explosive 1v1 dribbling ability in isolated scenarios against Uruguay's wider defenders.
